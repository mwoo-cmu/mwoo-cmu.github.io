CANTONESE: A Comprehensive Grammar

CANTONESE: A Comprehensive Grammar is a digitised textbook, with various webpages discussing grammar. They have Chinese characters with Yale romanisation, and each example has an MP3 you can listen to.

第dai6二 ji6個 go3網 mong5頁 jip6

FSI Cantonese

FSI Cantonese has two e-books, with Chinese characters and Yale romanisation, and you can listen to the lessons. They use the 7 tones, so they separate out the 生 saang˥ in 學生 hok6 saang1 (high level) from the 生 saang˥˧ in 先生 sin1 saang1 (high falling).

Cantonese Learning has a lot of lessons, including grammar and vocabulary. They also have videos to watch and songs to listen to.

Modified Basic Cantonese

This book talks about Cantonese grammar; they have a lot of examples in Jyutping and Chinese characters.

Open Cantonese

Open Cantonese has a few free course books, going over elementary Cantonese, as well as the Jyutping and Yale romanisation schemes. They also have a library of community resources, and a Jyutping annotator, though it is sometimes wrong for words with multiple pronunciations.

Learn Cantonese

Learn Cantonese has quite a few vocabulary lists, but they don't use Jyutping, and they have a lot of words which they do not include the Chinese words for; they only have the pronunciation. I think it has value, but you will need to learn a new romanisation scheme.

Cantonese Class 101 has 9 free webpages going over introductory material, grammar, and Chinese characters. They also have some practice exercises you can do.

Lingora (Android and Apple)

This app has a lot of lessons using sentences to help you practice. I think it's good, but at the start there is only one sentence, so the exercises are all the same. If you already know some Cantonese, you still need to start from lesson 1, and there are some exercises that require you to listen.

Clozemaster

This webpage has a lot of fill-in-the-blanks questions that you can do, but there are some where I feel that I would not phrase it the way they did. The free tier allows you to do 30 sentences per day, and a few small paragraphs.

Open Yue Dictionary Collection Platform

Open Yue Dictionary Collection Platform has multiple dictionaries you can use to search for words. They include Cantonese, Hoisanese and Kaipingnese dictionaries. Because they just aggregate these different dictionaries, the words' definitions may be in Cantonese, English, or Mandarin.

Words.HK (CantoWords.com)

Words.HK has a lot of Cantonese words, and has definitions in both English and Chinese, so it can be used to practice reading. However, there are a lot of non-publicly-available words, so you have to support on Patreon to see them. Chinese, English, and Jyutping can be used to search for words. The English version allows you to see the first sentence of non-publicly-available definitions, but the Chinese version just says Not publicly available.

Cantonese+ Words and Sentences

Cantonese+ has word, phrase, and sentence collections, and you can use various different ways to filter the words/phrases/sentences—other dictionaries only let you use one approach to search. I was also able to find words I couldn't elsewhere. The sentences largely come from Words.HK.
